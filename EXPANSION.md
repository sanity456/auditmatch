# Expansion route

## Phase 1 — Evidence connectors

Add normalized connectors for GitHub report repositories, Code4rena, Sherlock, Immunefi, package registries, and auditor-controlled `.well-known` manifests. The goal is higher-quality evidence and lower application friction, not a larger opaque score.

## Phase 2 — Operator analytics

Offer foundations and security councils private dashboards for time-to-shortlist, stack coverage, evidence freshness, repeat applicants, vendor diversity, and unfilled specialist gaps. This is the lowest-risk commercial layer.

## Phase 3 — Private scope commitments

Commit hashes for confidential scope documents and use selective disclosure or encrypted off-chain data rooms. Keep secrets and embargoed vulnerabilities out of public contract state.

## Phase 4 — Escrow adapters

Deploy a separate, independently audited escrow that consumes `evaluate_policy_view`, records mutual engagement acceptance, and releases milestone payments. Keep AuditMatch itself non-custodial.

## Phase 5 — Appeal bonds

Introduce bonds only after real contest data reveals who appeals, why, and how often. Specify escalation tiers, evidence windows, and slashing rules before attaching economic consequences.

## Phase 6 — Portable track record

Issue opt-in, purpose-scoped credentials for completed engagements and verified remediation. Avoid one permanent universal auditor score; specialties and evidence freshness matter more than a global rank.

## Recommended commercial order

1. Evidence templates and connectors.
2. Program analytics and procurement workflow subscriptions.
3. A conservative escrow pilot with capped value.
4. Appeal bonds based on observed dispute patterns.
5. Portable reputation once credential semantics are stable.
